GenAI in GTM - 6 months later

July 25, 2024 • 6 min read • GenAI in GTM

It's been 6 months since I embarked on a journey to leverage GenAI in GTM (Go-To-Market - sales, marketing, post-sales etc.). ChatGPT was all the rage, the hype was high and I wanted to find practical applications of GenAI in GTM. So where do things stand 6 months later? Have I found practical use cases? Are these making true business impact or is it a nice to have? Is it delivering to the promised value of productivity?

The first thing I realized was that while people talk about GenAI as one thing, GenAI is not 1 product. You are not going to be able to get very far just using ChatGPT and I think this is what most people expect. They will give their teams ChatGPT or Microsoft CoPilot and everyone has GenAI.

So, If you truly want to have business impact with GenAI you will need to go beyond ChatGPT and you will need to invest in more than 1 product. This is a challenge as most RevOps teams are focused on tech consolidation. They are trying to reduce the number of products in their tech stack but GenAI is going to require additional investments.

Another challenges is that the rate of change is so high (with new features / products etc. being released constantly) that organizations need to be comfortable experimenting. This too is an issue as organizations want the easy button - "just give me something that works." Most people don't like running experiments, because some of them fail, some of them you have to pivot and some succeed. With employees stretched thin doing their day jobs, who has time to experiment. Plus experimentation is a different mindset.

With this context, if organizations are still interested in getting value from GenAI, the first thing people need to understand is that there are different product options in front of them. I love this visual and have used it before.

You have 4 types of product options -

  1. LLMs - ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini etc. You give it prompts and it generates content for you. The only UI is a chat interface. This is what most people associate with GenAI.

  2. Feature in existing products - All your existing SaaS tech stack vendors are investing in GenAI features. This is an easy way to get acclimated to GenAI. Some vendors make this super easy, they've put guardrails and have removed some of the knowledge of prompt engineering that is needed and others have bolt on features that are not as intuitive.

  3. Point Solutions - These are companies that have built from the ground up on GenAI and don't have the legacy architecture that existing companies are stuck with. They provide the fastest time to value as they have all the functionality out of the box and you don't have to figure out prompting, load the data, figure out integrations etc.

  4. Agent Builders - There are tools that let you build your own workflows or applications that leverage GenAI + Automation + Integrations. They are extremely configurable and flexible and can help meet any needs you have to apply GenAI to your business processes. It does require you to be an expert in prompting and process design.

So, if you are a GTM leader and are wondering how you can leverage GenAI, here are some business areas where you can get real and tangible value. The value here is along the lines of - a person with GenAI will do the work of multiple people without GenAI.

While I am referencing some products here I will reiterate that there are probably 5 other products that do something similar. So not saying this is exhaustive, just that there are solutions out there that you can use today.

Enablement - An enablement person using Letter.ai (point solution) or AnyQuest.ai (Agent Builder) will handle the workload of multiple enablement people. Letter.ai will be much more full featured and out of the box (faster time to value) than building your own agents. You can also use LLMs and use the chat interface to generate the content you need. I assume vendors like Highspot will start providing some capabilities. So if you are a resource strapped enablement team and you are constantly being asked to build content (battlecards, competitive docs, lesson plans, role plays, assessments etc.), have you considered GenAI to reduce your workload?

SDR - An SDR using Clay.com (Agent Builder) will do the work of multiple SDRs. Clay integrates with various tools in your tech stack thus allowing you to orchestrate your lead gen workflows. What makes leveraging GenAI in SDR workflows is that this requires a combination of GenAI + integrations. Most SDRs access multiple systems - a sales engagement platform, a CRM, a data vendor, LinkedIn SalesNav etc. If all you have is ChatGPT, you will have a very fragmented workflow where ChatGPT can do a piece of the workflow but you will spend a lot of time flipping between tools. Clay (and similar tools) have the pre-built integrations with lots of providers and thus can automate large parts of the SDR workflow. Now you add GenAI to generate personalized messages, do research etc. and you have a more full featured solution.

Product Marketing - A PMM using Tribyl.com (Point Solution) will do the work of multiple PMMs. Tribyl does need access to Gong, Chorus or Clari calls as that is the raw data it leverages. You could also leverage AnyQuest.ai (Agent Builder) to automate some aspects and get started, but the onus is on you to build out all the various workflows, figure out the prompts, organize the content etc. You will still need to feed it calls. You can also use LLMs and use the chat interface to get the output you need but this will provide the most fragmented experience and lack repeatability.

Customer Success - This one is trickier as the underlying data that is needed for Customer Success is all over the place. There is structured data in systems like Gainsight and CRM etc. There is unstructured data in Slack, documents etc. The one use case I have found is to generate Handoff documents from call recordings. It comes back to call recordings, but if you have those, you can feed them to GenAI and have it generate a hand off document. Your choices here are to build repeatable agents with a tool like AnyQuest.ai or feed the calls to an LLM and have it generate a hand off document or use the Deals Ask Me feature in Gong.io and have Gong generate the various sections of a handoff document. Generating QBR decks are trickier as we need to get access to structured data like ROI / Adoption / Usage etc. (you can generate some parts of a QBR deck).

Bottomline, if you are a GTM leader and are open to investing in GenAI technologies, you can absolutely get value from GenAI today and knowing that most supporting teams are trying to do more with less, remember a person with GenAI will be way more productive than one without. Start experimenting rather than waiting.
