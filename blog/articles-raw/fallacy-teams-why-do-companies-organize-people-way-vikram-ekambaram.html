The fallacy of teams - Why do companies organize people the way they do?

Created on 2019-05-02 10:50

Published on 2019-05-02 11:33

After a long long hiatus spending my free time watching shows and movies, playing video games, doubling down on tennis I finally picked up a book the other night. It was a business book around the concept of an Ideal Team Player. The author (Patrick Lencioni) does a great job of storytelling and a couple hours later and a little after midnight I was done reading the book. Like a lot of other business books I read, this book left my brain buzzing with ideas. Things I could do different, things I could improve. In fact I was so energized (sometimes the thirsty man does not realize how parched he was) that I downloaded the other book written by the author (The five dysfunctions of a team) and before I knew it, it was 3 am and I had finished that book too. 

I've spent a lot of time thinking about the ideas in the book over the last couple weeks and it led me to the topic of this post - "Why are companies organized the way they are". I work for a software company and we are organized like any other software company - Sales reports into a Sales leader, PreSales into a PreSales leader, Field Marketing into a Field Marketing leader, Customer Success into a Customer Success leader, Services into a Services leader, Partner team into a Partner leader etc.

One thing all these teams have in common is that they are customer facing and if we boil it down we are all executing towards driving a better customer experience. Relentless customer focus is part of our DNA. That being said the way we are organized leads to people in a 'team' not really working together as a 'team' to drive towards that goal of relentless customer focus. We tend to segment our customers and assign each team member a group of customers to work on. So most of the time my 'team' is not working as a team in terms of driving a better customer experience they are part of a virtual team supporting the customer.

If we look at examples of true teams - let's say a sports team or the Avengers etc. they are all what we would call cross functional and are all executing towards a common goal. Imagine a basketball team that had a Point Guard coach with a bunch of Point Guards, a Center coach with a bunch of Centers, a Power Forward coach with a bunch of Power Forwards, you get the idea. All the practices happen with Centers practicing with Centers and so on. Each person masters their skill independently. That being said when games are played you have Center A, Point Guard A, Power Forward A etc. all playing together with the goal of winning the game (Sales AE, SC, CSM etc. working together towards driving a better customer experience). To take this one step further, we compensate everyone differently. Imagine the point guard was paid based on how many passes he made, the Center how many rebounds etc. Individually those make perfect sense but when you are in the game we can see how ridiculous this is.

This was when it hit me - there are so many business books around building teams, building team cultures, design thinking, agile, management, motivation etc. and what they say makes complete sense, but if we continue organizing functions the way we do, we end up with a lack of culture, lack of execution, people working towards cross purposes etc.

So the simplistic solution is very obvious. If your goal is to win a game (sports team), beat the bad guy (Avengers), be customer obsessed then organize around that common goal. So pick a set of customers and assign them an Account Exec, an SC, a CSM, a Partner manager, a Field Marketing person, a Services person etc. Compensate them on the outcome (the analogy of winning the game or customer retention / expansion / satisfaction etc.) and you will get a strong team culture because are working towards a common purpose, you will get teams that execute, work well together. Are there examples where this has been done in business, I am sure there are many. The ones that comes to mind are Agile teams / Design thinking projects / Scrum. Then why don't we do this around customer facing roles?

There are a couple downsides I can think of - firstly each team will end up doing what is right for their customers (nothing wrong with this) but you will have variations in what is being done, so there will be lack of consistency. Is that necessarily a bad thing? Different basketball teams play differently? You will have some customers suffer because you will have some dysfunctional teams. It throws the headcount model out of the window as often companies have lots of sales people, fewer SCs, fewer still CSMs, fewer still Field marketing folks. This would require a readjustment in headcount ratios, but would it lead to better execution?

How far do you take this? Do you bring in the development org and add product managers and developers to these teams? It would lead to exceptional customer experience as each customer would get the product they want but it won't scale. You'd have a nightmare support experience, since each customer has one off products built for them. Which brings me to the final point, this structure falls apart at scale unless you can really put the governance structures in place to make sure each team is properly trained and that there is consistency / commonality in place to execute and yes there is flexibility in execution but it can't be a bunch of one off teams doing their own thing.

I am curious what others have seen work.
