Tableau Blueprint - Your Guide to a Health Lifestyle

Created on 2020-03-10 16:39

Published on 2020-03-10 19:22

I have been on a year long health kick and tie that in to all my work around Tableau Blueprint and I could not resist writing this post. So about a year ago, I started getting elbow pain and it affected my tennis game. That started a year long quest to get healthier that has had me try out - a plant based diet (Gamechangers got me on that kick), that morphed into vegetarianism and then into a pescatarian diet. Charles Duhigg's How To podcast around dealing with pain introduced me to cold showers and Wim Hof. Yes, I now only take cold showers and I lasted the NY winter!! I stopped playing video games on my phone and changed my pillow. I do 30 mins of stretching every day, got a kneeling chair to fix my posture, a vertical mouse to put less pressure on my arm and on and on and on.

I recently wrote a post about Tableau Blueprint and why it was so important to start with understanding why. As I was thinking about what would make someone take cold showers in winter, stretch for 30 mins every day, change diet completely it came down to - I had a really good reason. My WHY was that I wanted to be healthy so I could play as much tennis as I wanted and I was willing to go to any lengths. Yes tennis means that much. My MEASURE of success was being able to play tennis 4 days a week and not get hurt. My support group, the WHO, that would keep me on track included my wife, my tennis buddies, my team in DC that loved the avocado/banana smoothies I would make when I visited. The HOW was a combination of being intentional about what I put in my body (intake) ate, drank etc., working out that included exercise, stretching and strengthening and lastly a healthy lifestyle that included breathing, sleeping and having a hobby that was driving me. Lastly, WHAT I did to execute the HOW, was all the stuff I mentioned above (plant based diet, minimize sugar, drink a celery/cilantro/ginger/apple juice drink every morning, stretch 30 mins every day, stretch before and after exercise, get 8 hours of sleep, do breathing exercises etc.).

So how is this relevant to Tableau Blueprint (apart from the fact that I redid the subway stops with one around creating a healthy lifestyle) is that in both cases the journey is long, hard, deals with a tough change management, takes time, costs money etc.

When we talk to customers about Blueprint, often the push back we get is around the fact that it seems like a lot of work, that it is going to take more resources and cost money. The fact is, all of that is true but for those customers that understand the competitive advantage of creating a data driven culture, making these investments is definitely worth it. We have large customers that executed all the elements in Blueprint before Blueprint existed, becuse they were clear on their WHY.

Sometimes we have customers that will start something on the right side of the Blueprint but then they get busy or can't get additional funding or can't enroll other resources etc and their plan to scale out Tableau sputters. This reminds me of the many times I have signed up to a gym and then dropped out or started a diet and then gave up or when I did not want to go to a personal trainer because it was too expensive and then got hurt because I was lifting weights the wrong way etc. or bought some new exercise gadget and never used it.

One important point on this topic is that sometimes organizations are not clear on the WHY and it takes doing some activities on the right to get clarity on the WHY. So, as an example, there is nothing wrong in starting with building a Community as that can help organizations see the benefit of creating a data culture and sometimes we need something tangible to get people to start believing in the WHY. So don't assume that if the customer is not clear on their WHY, it means we don't do anything. It just means that we need to be intentional about using the activities to get the customer to start understanding WHY. Bottomline, when someone is clear about WHY they will be willing to do crazy stuff like taking cold showers in the winter!! So sooner we get them to be clear on the WHY the sooner we will see them getting success in their journey to creating a data culture.

To conclude - is it working? The short answer is that it has been a learning experience. The diet changes did not help much. In fact, I have hypothyroidism and the diet changes seem to make that worse. So I've had to back down on the diet changes. The cold showers and stretching definitely helped and I started playing more. But then I started playing too much and got hurt again. So I am back on the mend. Guess I need to do a lot more strengthening to be able to sustain the level of tennis I want to play. The kneeling chair completely got rid of my shin splints. The new pillow got rid of my neck and shoulder pain. So overall, many steps forward and some steps sideways which is exactly how creating a data culture at many organizations is!!
