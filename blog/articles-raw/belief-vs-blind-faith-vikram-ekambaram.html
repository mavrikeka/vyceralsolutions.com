Belief vs Blind Faith

Created on 2015-11-30 13:24

Published on 2015-11-30 14:05

As an organization, the importance of getting people (employees / customers etc.) to share your beliefs has been identified as a key to better engagement and success. Daniel Pink, Simon Sinek etc. have all talked about this. 

The question that comes up for me is - how do you get people to believe in what you believe. Simon Sinek talks about 'Start with the Why'. Explain to people 'why' you do 'what' you do. While this is critical, what if people don't align with your 'Why'? Daniel Pink talks about the study done by Adam Grant around call center employees at a university fund raising organization where you listen to stories of the beneficiaries, to understand the impact the fundraising was having. This too gets back to 'Why was the university raising funds and what impact it was having'.

When I joined Tableau, I realized something was different. We help people see and understand data, because we believe that people are creative and the right tool can unleash human ingenuity. While I wanted to believe in this, it was still abstract. As I met with customers and heard their stories, each story further cemented this core belief. What was missing was - so how does this help me be successful. Being in sales that meant making my number. Did I really believe that by enabling customers to be successful and unleashing human ingenuity, I would be successful.

I remember the first half of the year was spent in what I'll call the 'Blind Faith' period. I did not know if I'd be successful but I was willing to give it a try. The key was being surrounded by a community that supported me and that I could turn to when I had doubts (and I had lots of doubts). The second half of the year, I saw the results and I became a believer. So while 'Belief' was important, I didn't start with 'Belief', I just had to take the leap.

That being said, sales is one job that really makes you question your beliefs and tests that commitment to that belief. It's a new year, you have a bigger quota and you are again unsure. It comes back to having that community to support you and keep you believing. What I have learnt is that when my 'Belief' is wavering  I make sure I am not alone. Community is key.

Just like community can help cement your belief, it can also erode the belief very quickly. We've all been in situations where we join a new company and we are motivated / engaged but the water cooler talk is so negative that soon any excitement we have is extinguished. I guess the moral is to surround yourself with people who believe what you believe.
