Disruption - Characteristics needed in Leaders

Created on 2019-05-10 16:06

Published on 2019-05-10 16:13

Disruption is around us. Change is happening fast and those who stand still get passed. Laws, rules, regulations are one way to slow this down but not sure they work in the long run. This being the case, what characteristics should leaders demonstrate to be successful in a disruptive world? Here are some I thought of, interested in ideas as I'm sure there are many that I have missed -

  1.  Ability to organize chaos - If there is one thing that is a given about disruption, old ways of doing things don't work. So the stability that existed is gone. What is left is chaos which can be seen as an opportunity to create a new way or a threat to the old way of doing things. Leaders have to be able to make sense of the chaos, understand key elements of the path forward and communicate this clearly to their teams. Without this clear direction, teams will all do their own thing and some will hunker down and resist the change while others will stand still and wait for clarity and some will forge new ways that may or may not work. The challenge for leaders is that sometimes they are unsure of what that clear direction for the path forward is.
  2.  Be execution focused - In a disruption, there is one guarantee a lot of strategies won't work. A lot of strategies are created based on how things used to be and a lot of strategies are created by people still stuck with legacy thinking. Also, a lot of strategies have to be created based on lack of information as no one knows how things will turn out. In such a situation, leaders that are close to the execution will be the ones that are fastest to respond. They will experiment, listen to the signals, fail fast and keep pivoting till they find the right strategy.
  3.  Be data driven - Experimenting without data creates solutions based on hope and gut feel. You try something and you get lucky. You don’t know why it worked or how it worked. Leaders need to be data driven and this does not just mean having analysts / machine learning etc. feeding them answers to their questions. Yes, signals being sent are critical but Leaders have to also be curious about their data. A lot of answers lie in the data and a lot of false hopes can be prevented with data. They have to play with the data, ask lots and lots of questions and understand analytical concepts. They need to get their hands dirty with the data.
  4.  Be trusted - Lastly, leaders need to be trusted by their teams. They will be taking their teams in unchartered directions and they need their teams fully committed. People will be asking the question 'why change', 'why now', 'why should I listen to you' and a leader that is not trusted will get 'head nods' and 'fake yeses' without a commitment from their teams to change. This in turn will doom any change in strategy and it will not get executed.
