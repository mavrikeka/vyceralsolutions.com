People power - but what does a manager do?

Created on 2015-11-01 22:17

Published on 2015-11-01 22:50

I work for a company that empowers everyone to make decisions based on data. This is the main reason for our success. Our product also allows individuals to experiment, be curious and fail fast. The investment required is pretty low. This means that individuals are motivated and make a difference in the organization. It also makes the organization more nimble in reacting to external pressures.

In case you are wondering what is wrong with the above picture - it does leave a group of people (typically IT) who have traditionally filled the role of analyzing data wondering what their role is.

Change is happening so rapidly, that everything is getting disrupted. Even traditional org structures and roles. 

In organizations, decisions and strategies have typically been decided at the top and then pushed down to managers to implement and individual contributors (ICs) to execute. As we all know this approach does not always work. Managers end up having to use a stick or a carrot approach, which doesn't motivate the ICs. The ICs feel they don't necessarily understand 'Why' they are being asked to do whatever is expected of them and it creates a friction between managers and individuals. In the end, ICs don't execute to the strategy and the strategy fails. Hard to get belief with a carrot or stick approach.

Organizations now realize that the alternative approach is to explain the 'Why', get people to believe in it and let the individuals come up with the 'What' (with the proper guidance). This works great as individuals are much more motivated and empowered. The ICs are also on the ground and closest to the problem, so they can come up with solutions that are localized and relevant and also change quickly when the situation changes. This creates an organization of problem solvers, which in turns is a competitive advantage. Lastly, ICs will adopt the solution, because they came up with the solution.

So what is a manager's role?

My take is that one of the key roles of the manager is to facilitate and remove obstacles. Firstly, they need to create a team that is comfortable with the new power that has been given to them. Secondly, even when employees are motivated and empowered, invariably there are systemic issues that will prevent them from succeeding. The role of the manager becomes critical in understanding the systemic issues and solving them.

For example, in a sales organization ICs might come up with a solution to revive flagging sales. They believe in the solution and it aligns with the organizations 'Why'. Now, let's say that the compensation is set up in such a way that the ICs get penalized if they implement that solution (they don't get commission). Chances are the ICs will not do things the new way.

The role of the manager is to fix the compensation issue. If we believe that we hire great people, the role of the manager should not be the 'manager' but instead be a remover of obstacles.

Just like the data analysis example, where the role of IT goes from 'report creator' to a business enabler (provide business access to data so they can analyze it themselves), I would hazard a guess that managers would much rather focus on facilitating and removing obstacles, than wield a big stick. Similar to a better business-IT alignment, we could see a much better manager-employee alignment.
