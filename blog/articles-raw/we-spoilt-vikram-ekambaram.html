Are we spoilt?

Created on 2016-01-26 11:59

Published on 2016-01-27 23:55

I'm a big believer in Thomas Pink's assessment that motivation is based not just on money but also a combination of autonomy, purpose and mastery. Lately, another question has been popping up in my mind - are we spoilt? Is asking for autonomy, purpose and mastery just a way for the have's to want more?

I was looking at youth unemployment data from the World Bank and the numbers were pretty scary - Spain / Greece have 50+% youth unemployment. So on the one hand we have folks with a stable job and a good income who are not motivated because they don't have 'autonomy, purpose and mastery', on the other hand we have kids with no jobs.

The other day I took a cab to the airport and I was thinking about the life of a cab driver. Morning till evening he drives people to and from places. Is his job really 'motivating'? It is probably just a means to an end - to provide for his family. Given a choice wouldn't he rather do something else? Does he really care about Thomas Pink?

As humans we seem to be wired to want more rather than being satisfied with what we have. Which probably explains why we innovate and explore and try new things. If we were all satisfied with the status quo, there would be no urgency to do something different and change. We'd probably go extinct. 

The flip side of wanting more is that whenever we raise the bar, we get used to the new normal. After the initial excitement of having reached our goal, we want more. It is like a video game where you work hard to get to the next level. You are all excited when you get to the next level. That excitement lasts for a little while and then you want more. We tend to forget where we started, where we came from (the past), we enjoy the present for a fleeting moment and then we are on a chase to get to the future. Soon the future is the present and we are off chasing the next future.

Where is that balance between being thankful for what you have and craving for more?
