If Willpower is the key to Success, then Distraction is the key to Willpower

Created on 2015-12-24 02:57

Published on 2015-12-24 03:30

There have been studies and articles about how people with more willpower are more successful than those with less willpower. There is a very famous experiment called the Marshmallow Test that was first conducted in the 60s that kicked off a lot of research on this topic. As we head into the holidays and we all get ready to give in to the various temptations of over-eating and sweets, I thought writing a post about willpower would be fitting.

I've been doing a spinning class lately and there are many moments where my muscles are burning and I just want to give up. The more I try to ignore the pain, the more my mind focuses on the pain and the more I want to give up. Soon it is unbearable and I take it down a notch and give in to the easy option.

The other day, something strange happened. I was a bit distracted and thinking about a blog post around community, habit and spinning that I wanted to write. Sure enough as the intensity of the exercise picked up, my muscles started to burn and I starting zeroing in on the pain, but then my mind started thinking about my blog post and next thing I realized was that a few minutes had gone by with me lost in thought and cycling unconsciously. I had forgotten the pain and I was able to keep the pace up. This happened a few more times and I realized that while my muscles hurt, it was my brain that was making me give up. The moment I gave my brain something else to think about, it stopped focusing on the pain and I completely forgot the pain and I was able to continue.

So what I've started doing now is to recite the Fibonacci series in my head anytime my brain starts thinking about the pain. Since the brain is single threaded, and solving a math problem takes up all its focus, I am able to forget about my muscles. It stops telling me to give up. Which made me realize that the reason we don't have self-control or willpower is often because we focus too much on the thing we want to avoid and the more we try to avoid it, the more we focus on it and that is a losing battle.

The solution is to distract your mind so you then have the willpower to resist the temptation. So the next time you see a cookie and you can't resist it, the best thing to do is to look away, think about something else, do a math problem, recite a song, talk to the person next to you etc. Anything to distract your brain and hopefully the moment will pass and someone else will eat the cookie :-)
