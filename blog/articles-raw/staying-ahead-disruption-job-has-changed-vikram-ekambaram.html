Staying ahead of disruption - The job has changed

Created on 2019-08-08 12:43

Published on 2019-08-08 13:36

I'd written a post a couple weeks ago about staying ahead of disruption (here) and that focused on the disruption that we all face in our day to day jobs and a mindset to respond to the disruption. As I spent more time thinking about this I came to the realization that the disruption that we face in our jobs is often gradual. Like the frog in the boiling water, by the time we realize it is too late.

It got me to thinking about really good people at Tableau that went from being rock stars to suddenly being on the sidelines. How does this happen? The questions I have been asking are -

  • Did these rock stars suddenly stop wanting to be rock stars?
  • Did they change in some fundamental way?
  • Did the job change?

What I've realized is that it wasn't them, it was the job. Like the frog analogy, over time the job changed because of external disruption forces and these rock stars, kept executing to the old job and suddenly the results were not there or the results based on the old measurement were there but the new measure of success was not being met. New leadership came in that saw the disruption and made the changes needed but people who were set in their ways ('I've been successful, don't tell me how to do things', 'You don't get it, this is how it works' etc.) just could not accept that the job had changed and expectations were different.

So what is the learning here -

  1. When the job changes we have a choice, we can go all in on the expectations of the new job or we can choose to do something different. This isn't a bad thing as sometimes the new disrupted world is not what we signed up for and we are better off moving on.
  2. We can't hold on to the old way and resist the new direction otherwise we end up like the frog. This is probably not a good thing as it leads to stress to everyone concerned.
  3. Can you anticipate the disruption and change the job? So instead of being the frog can you be the one controlling the temperature.

My 0.02, I often forget that I don't have to be the frog and let someone else control the temperature. What if I proactively looked for signals of disruption and as Mahatma Gandhi said - "be the change you wish to see in the world". The other option is to be a fast follower. Sometimes it is very hard to see the disruption when I am the one being disrupted but I need to develop hyper awareness to signals so I see the disruption and react quickly. This key to this is to be data driven. Without data (e.g. water temperature is rising) we are flying blind. Lastly, if both of the above don't work I need to remind myself to embrace change and any time I find myself saying 'this is too hard', 'this is how we've done it', 'this is why it won't work' or some variation of the expert bias, I need to reset my mindset.
