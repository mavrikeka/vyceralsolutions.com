Sales Negotiation - What I learned from my mother-in-law

Created on 2015-11-11 14:02

Published on 2015-11-11 14:32

When things get a little contentious my mother-in-law has a saying that you catch more flies with honey than with vinegar. Now we all know that you catch fruit flies with vinegar, but the point is that you will get more with kindness than being confrontational.

How is this related to sales negotiations. Well, most negotiations tend to end up being contentious. One side wants a bigger discount, wants better terms, is stuck on some legality etc. Usually people start getting uncomfortable. Often you have procurement or legal making demands of the salesperson and the salesperson is stuck between what the customer wants and what will get approved internally.

Invariably the customer asks for something 'extra' and the salesperson finds it easier to turn to his legal team or management and ask for that term to be approved or the higher discount to be approved and the internal discussions become contentious. We've all been in such situations where we are stuck in the middle between getting the customer to agree to what we want and our internal folks to agree to what the customer wants.

What I've realized is that if I understand the 'Why' behind the request from the customer or the response from my internal team, it makes the conversation go much easier. To me the 'Why' becomes the honey. When you tell people 'No, this is how it is' that is vinegar and people immediately put up their guard and it becomes contentious.

For example -

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Vinegar - We don't discount. This is our policy.

Customer reaction - Screw you. Who do you think you are? I've negotiated big discounts with other vendors.

Honey - Our policy is not to discount because we believe in the value our software provides. To prove this I am happy to give you a free trial and help you get started. I know our competitors are cheaper, but I recommend you try using their software too before you buy.

Customer reaction - hmm.. let me think about this.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Vinegar - I need to give more discount to my customer. The competition is at half our price.

Management - No. We don't ever discount that heavily. (they're thinking, this sales guy only wants discounts).

Honey - We have a situation where this use case is not in our sweet spot. Our competitor has a better solution and is willing to discount. If we do win this it allows us to become a standard. We have 2 options - we can walk or we can offer them a 1 time discount. What should we do?

Management reaction - hmm.. let me think about this.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I've found that understanding the 'Why' also makes us better listeners and trusted by both internal teams and the customer. Everyone has a reason why they want what they want, it just takes time to understand those reasons. I've found this especially true with legal stuff. What to me as a sales guy seems arbitrary and where my reaction is - "they are not helping me with my deal", switches when I take the time to get to the 'Why'. Sometimes you find out there is no good reason and then it helps to reframe the discussion.
