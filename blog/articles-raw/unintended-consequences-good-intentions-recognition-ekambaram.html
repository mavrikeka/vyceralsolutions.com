The Unintended Consequences of Good Intentions - Public Recognition

Created on 2020-02-23 18:22

Published on 2020-02-23 19:17

The 'Unintended Consequences of Good Intentions' has been something I have wanted to blog about for a long time. In business, home, tennis etc. I am constantly reminded of how something that started out with the right intentions sometimes ends up with not so great outputs. We just finished our annual sales kick off and again I was reminded of this powerful statement. So I thought I'd take some time to go over this topic and try to decipher the 'why' behind it and solicit ideas on what we could do differently.

Recognition is very important in organizations (and elsewhere). People want to know that the work they are doing is having an impact. People want to know that they are having an impact. In this HBS article from last year the author calls out that “What really matters in the workplace is helping employees feel appreciated.” I am lucky that I work for a company that deeply cares about its employees and understands the impact that each and every person has and makes the effort to recognize folks.

The start of a new year is always a great time to recognize folks / teams that have had great impact. Annual Company Kick Off events are a great venue to provide public recognition. We just finished our Sales Kick Off and there was a lot of recognition and awards last week. The people who got recognized absolutely deserved the awards they got. They had had standout performances. In addition, unlike some companies where the recognition is only about the output (who made their number, who hit their target) we went out of the way to also recognize folks that had the right inputs but might not have got the outputs. We all know those folks that are doing all the right things but for various reasons, did not make their number or hit their targets.

Despite all this goodness, there was an undercurrent of folks feeling left out. Whether it was in the form of a particular group not being represented for an award or an individual feeling they were deserving but did not get recognized or a manager forgetting a team member. The specifics of the situation are not that important but instead it is this unintended consequence (people feeling left out) of something good (public recognition) that I want to tease out. To be fair, there is probably a large chunk of folks that were not affected but instead left pumped to win some awards the next year but it does leave some people feeling 'excluded'.

So why did this happen? It wasn't because people were trying to hurt others. In fact it was the opposite. So I have some theories and would love to hear from others -

  • You cannot recognize everyone despite the fact that in a high performing organization a lot of people are very deserving. Otherwise the recognition loses value (like every kid getting a medal for participation). So this means there will always be folks that will feel left out. So the hypothesis can be that the positive impact on the folks that are recognized and folks that leave pumped up / motivate, outweighs the negative impact on the folks that felt left out.
  • When awards are not 'data driven' it can leave people feeling left out. For example, if a team gets an award for being the top performing team, there is no argument. It is based on data but if the same award is subjective, it is open to interpretation.
  • For 'subjective' awards, if the reason behind the choice is not clearly explained, it can lead to perceptions of unfairness.
  • If there is a group with let's say 7 teams and individuals from 6 teams get recognized and 1 team does not, it is easy for that 7th team to feel like their effort did not matter.
  • When people don't invest the time in really thinking through the recognition, hastily put together a write up or forget someone, the unintended consequences are higher than the recognition.

So what can we learn from this -

  • Be as 'data driven' as possible.
  • If it is going to be subjective, take a lot more time making sure to consider the unintended consequences.
  • While you cannot recognize everyone, having a list of nominees provides an alternate way to recognize more people. Recognition is not just about winning the award.
  • Invest a lot more time in recognition planning, creating detailed write ups, making sure someone or a team is not being left out unintentionally.
  • If there are too many awards then from a manager standpoint it becomes difficult to be intentional about the awards but despite that, managers need to take the time.
  • Spread the awards through the year, so there isn't a rush at the end when managers are busy with closing out the year, planning for the new year etc.

Wondering if this is just me or do others feel this way.
