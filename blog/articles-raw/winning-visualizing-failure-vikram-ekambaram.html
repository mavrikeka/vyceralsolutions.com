Winning by visualizing failure

Created on 2015-12-11 00:17

Published on 2015-12-11 02:56

A few weeks ago I was playing a tennis match and things were not going well. The opponents were pretty good and as the first set progressed it definitely looked like I was going to lose. Every mistake I made the voice in my head would tell me that I was going to lose. I tried to be positive and to banish the negative thoughts, but we all know how difficult it to break out of negativity while you are playing.

At one of the breaks in between service games as I sat down, I told myself I am going to lose. Ask any sports psychologist and this would be a big no-no as you don't want negativity to seep in (at least that is what I think - since I haven't actually asked a sports psychologist). I let the thought sink in that I was going to lose and then I spent a few more minutes thinking about what would happen if I lost. That immediately put things in perspective as I realized that winning or losing did not really mean anything. What happened next was that I suddenly stopped worrying and started playing. Like any good story that has to have a happy ending, I ended up winning the match.

As I think about the relevance of this to work, I am reminded of how over the last couple years, I have applied this principle to any new project I take on or new role I take on. At the beginning of last year, I was asked to manage some of our largest customers which also came with a pretty big jump in quota. I was new to Tableau and this was scary. At the beginning of this year, I was asked to take on a role with some of our largest partners. I had been in direct sales for 7 years and I would be doing something that wasn't clearly defined. In both cases, what helped me get past the paralysis of 'fear of failure' and 'playing safe' was asking myself the question - what is the worst that can happen? Just asking that question, put a lot of things in perspective. It made me pause and reflect on my strengths. It made me realize that I was the same person and just because I was trying something new / different, did not mean I was going to change or lose my abilities.

I think the biggest learning for me was that the end result (winning or losing or doing well or doing badly) did not really matter. What mattered was how I went about the job. I had to commit to doing my best. This seems true in so many levels. Most of our life is spent in the journey and you spend a few fleeting moments at the destination. So why do we put so much emphasis on the destination (winning or losing)? Life is lived in the journey and the best you can do is to enjoy that journey to the fullest. The beauty for me has been that at the end of each journey, I have ended up where I was meant to end up.

I was doing a presentation today and one of the comments that stuck in my mind was - yes Tableau is a tool that creates visualizations but what we really do is we take our customers on an analytical journey. A journey where they get over their fear of data, a journey where they learn to make mistakes and be curious, a journey where they unleash their potential and creativity. Again, it is not about the destination (visualization / dashboard you create).

It reminds me of the second half of that tennis match where I was in the flow and I was playing with freedom and unleashing my potential. Fear had been put on hold.
