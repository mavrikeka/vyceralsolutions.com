Does a "Culture of Self-Reliance" create a "Culture of Innovation"?

Created on 2016-08-08 14:15

Published on 2016-08-08 16:53

Innovation (The Big "I") seems to be the holy grail that every enterprise is striving for. As tech companies run circles around traditional companies and disrupt everything in sight, everyone is scrambling to be more Innovative. So how can a company be more innovative? Looking at the success of tech companies, one of the approaches is to create a "Culture of Innovation"? Don't lock up innovation within a core group but encourage grassroots innovation, as you never know where the next big idea is going to come from. Ideally, everyone in the organization should be responsible for innovation. So how does a company create a "Culture of Innovation"?

My mom is visiting from India and we were at the mall and walked into one of those "As seen on TV" stores. You know those stores that sell 'gadgets' - some are gimmicky but some are really useful in making life easier. A lot of them deal with stuff around the house. My mom was marveling on how it seemed that you could find such novelties, only in the US. At the same time she also mentioned that she had a cook that took care of cutting vegetables and cooking, a maid that took care of all the mopping and cleaning etc.

On another occasion, I was driving back from the store and I was eating a popsicle and my mom offered to hold the popsicle in case I needed to change the gear and I mentioned that the car was not a stick shift and I did not need 2 hands to steer and work the clutch. She again commented about how the American way seemed to be about making things easier. Now my mom hires a driver when she needs to get around town. Labor in India is pretty inexpensive so there is always someone you can pay to do stuff for you.

These 2 incidents got me to thinking why the US creates so many innovations and I think the difference is between India having a culture of outsourcing vs the US having a culture of self-reliance. A lot of the innovations are not that relevant to my mom as they would make the job of the maid or cook or driver easier. Since there is fixed labor cost, making them more efficient does not really deliver much value. On the other hand, since we have to do our own cooking, cleaning and driving in the US, anything that saves us time is very relevant. Also, a lot of these innovations are typically created by the end user.

My favorite innovation is the "Wolf Shovel". A total life saver if you live in the Northeast and have a 100 ft driveway that you shovel yourself. It was created by a guy in Connecticut  that felt there had to be a better way to shovel a driveway. I can guarantee that if he had to pay someone to shovel his driveway and not deal with back issues he would have never come up with this idea.

The company I work for disrupted the Business Intelligence market, because our CEO and founders got tired of dealing with spreadsheets when it came to analyzing data and ended up creating Tableau. Again, if they hadn't felt the pain first hand, they would never have come up with the idea.

So my position on this topic is that organizations need to focus on creating a culture of self-reliance if they want to be more innovative. While outsourcing (and even automation) can reduce your costs (hopefully), it can in some cases lead to reduced innovation. I am not advocating against outsourcing (or automation), but I think organizations need to look at what they are outsourcing (automating) and make sure that these are not areas that could lead to innovation. There has to be a strategy for both outsourcing/automating and self-reliance.
