Life lessons from a passion project

Created on 2020-08-20 13:56

Published on 2020-08-20 16:54

The last 2 months my 12 year old and I embarked on a passion project that highlighted so many life lessons that I just feel like I have to share our story. My son loves playing Fortnite and a couple months ago he decided to start playing it on our computer (and not just his Switch). The computer is about 5 years old and the graphics were really slow in loading etc. Overall, not a great experience with all the lags in the game.

He comes to me a couple months ago and says he wants a new graphics card. He'd done his research and figured out that that would solve his lag issues. My son is a lego kid and has always shown some interest in taking things apart and putting them together, but it isn't something he is particularly passionate about. In true dad style, I said let's open the computer and let us do some learning on the different parts of the computer. This theoretical endeavor went nowhere as 'this is the CPU and this is the hard drive and this is RAM' did not have as much excitement for him as it was for me to 'teach' him about computers.

So after that initial chat about needing a graphics card, I told him to figure out what kind of card and make sure it would work etc. So we spent a couple more weeks researching graphics cards and then he realized he needed a new power supply (PSU). Finally, he was ready to pull the plug and gave me the specific graphics card and power supply that needed to be bought. I placed the order and the day the parts arrived were met with great excitement.

My son opened up the computer, installed the new PSU and Graphics card and... the computer died. You press the switch and there was no sign of life. This then set in motion a journey that spanned figuring out how to test a PSU (paper clip test), how to take out the motherboard (breadboarding!!) and start from basics to find the root cause and fix it. Then Murphy's law struck. Once we figured out how to fix the motherboard and PSU and graphics card, the Hard Drive would not work.

This brought us to last weekend when my son said he was done. Everything that could go wrong had gone wrong and we should just take it to the shop. Luckily, I had just heard a podcast from HBR on the tipping point between success and failure was that successful people tried things just one more time. So we decided to take one more shot and we got it working.

This entire experience just brought home a lot of life lessons that I wanted to call out -

  • If you are passionate about something you will persevere in the face of challenges. You will demonstrate grit because you care about the outcome.
  • You learn a lot from struggle and failure. If my son had installed the graphics card and PSU and it had worked, that is all he'd have learnt. Them not working, led him to learn a whole lot of stuff. Now he can take apart any computer, troubleshoot it and fix it.
  • You learn by doing. My son would not have learnt what the different parts of the computer are, what they do, how they work if he had to read a text book or even just opened up the computer. He had an end in mind and that really turbocharged his learning.
  • The difference between success and failure is trying a little longer. One of the statements that stuck with me was - the difference between success and failure is 51 to 49 and not 100 to 0. Often we fail despite being so close.
  • Just passion to solve a problem is not enough. The fact that my son was open to tinkering meant this solution worked for him. So the passion has to align with your skills. If he had no interest in tinkering, we would have either bought a new computer or sent it to a shop or I'd have done it.
  • A growth mindset, trying new things and exploring can lead to unexpected goodness. My son learnt how to take apart a computer. I learnt how to take apart a computer. We got to bond over something.
  • Finishing something gives you a high that is hard to replace. Even if you finish a piece of the puzzle, you get a dopamine hit. The excitement my son felt when he was able to get the motherboard from not working to showing signs of life. When he got the hard drive to go from throwing an error to working. Each gave a burst of energy and hope that kept us going. So if you are on a long journey, define some intermediate milestones. It will keep you going.

Bottomline, it's been a great way to deal with COVID and my son can say he learnt how to build a computer!!
