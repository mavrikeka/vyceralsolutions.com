Did curiosity really kill the cat?

Created on 2015-10-23 18:18

Published on 2015-10-26 20:30

I've spent the large part of this year asking 'Why' whenever I get a chance. The reactions of people is always interesting. Some pause and actually think (you can see the wheels spinning) while others get defensive and yet others think I am annoying. It's been great to really understand what's going on and to get to the heart of any topic. It has also been great for communication as you are relevant and show empathy.

Which brings me to the topic of this post - what does 'Curiosity killed the cat' really mean? It's one of those proverbs that we have all heard growing and one that we all have used at some point of time. Are we basically saying, don't ask 'Why'? Don't ask too many questions? If you are curious, bad things will happen? It doesn't make any sense.

I did a little digging to see if wikipedia had a good answer and they did not - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curiosity_killed_the_cat

Then I checked YouTube - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0e_IXBR3jN0

I must say curious cats are funny and engaging. So I came to the conclusion that it was probably coined by some annoyed parent that got fed up by their kids asking 'Why, Why, Why'.

Curious what others think.
