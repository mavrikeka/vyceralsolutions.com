Negotiations - Where does the Vinegar come from?

Created on 2015-11-18 13:20

Published on 2015-11-18 13:57

I had recently written a post around negotiation tactics and using honey vs vinegar. This got me thinking about why some of my negotiations got contentious and I wanted to list some of the reasons. Would love to hear other opinions.

1. Making it personal - This for me was usually the biggest one. Anytime I forgot that this is a business deal and not about 'ME' vs procurement, it ended up getting contentious.

2. Getting emotional - Related to the point above, I would find that there were times I'd let my emotions get the better of me. I might not like my interactions with the person on the other side, maybe they were trying to get me upset and off keel (we are often dealing with people who do this for a living) etc. I would then need to remind myself of Supertramp - "Feeling so logical" (although I don't think the song meant that in a good way). Anytime I had a logical backing to my points, it was easy to step away from the emotion. Then the argument becomes about the data and your logic, rather than the emotion.

3. Getting defensive - This is because often I did not agree with the stance my management / legal was taking and I felt I had to convince the customer of something I did not believe in. My frustrations would show up in my negotiations. My response here was often to spend time with my management / legal and try to understand what the real issue was. Often I realized that I did not understand the implications of let's say - 'limited liability'. Once I got to that level of understanding, it made it easier for me to 'believe' and then communicate back. There were also times where it was just the way things were and I would be upfront with the customer that they were not going to like it, but it was our business policy. One of my managers had a great way of explaining this. He would say - "Mr customer, there are things we won't do and things we can't do. This is one of those things we 'won't do'. This means that none of our customers get this and it is not something that we are only making you agree to".

4. History - This I've found happens more with internal negotiations rather than external ones. We spend a lot of time getting internal buy in from management, legal etc. and over time the stances that are taken are more based on past interactions than the current situation. You tend to take the side of the customer and are usually asking for your legal to give in, without understanding the legal ramifications. Over time you have a reputation of 'giving in' and even if you have a valid reason, you will not be given the benefit of doubt. So the times when I feel that the response I am getting is not 'logical', I tend to take a step back and understand what is really going on and often I find it is something else. This is much more nuanced. You could run into a similar situation where a customer has had an experience negotiating with a prior rep and is treating you a certain way because of his past experience.

Love to hear other thoughts.
