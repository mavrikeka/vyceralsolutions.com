Staying ahead of disruption

Created on 2019-07-25 11:35

Published on 2019-07-25 12:22

The other day I was asked the question - "what drives you"? For the longest time I would respond with "doing my best". I have been described as very competitive ("wants to win") but I've always chafed at that because my competitiveness was always fueled by my need to "do my best". Not giving 100% always left me unsatisfied (I should caveat this by saying not giving 100% about stuff that I cared about left me unsatisfied). Winning or losing to me were outputs that I did not control but my level of effort was definitely something I owned.

The last couple years there has been a new element that has crept into this equation and that has been - "staying ahead of disruption'. I've blogged about disruption in the past. The pace of disruption is very high and everyone is affected by it. The challenge is that most people are not spending time watching for signs of disruption in their everyday lives. Most of us think about AirBnb disrupting the hotel industry, Uber disrupting taxi-drivers and it seems like we as consumers benefit from disruption and if we're lucky to not be in an industry affected by disruption then we are good.

The bad news is that disruption is all around us. I've been at Tableau almost 6 years and been relatively successful. The company has been quite successful and is seen as the disruptor in the Business Intelligence industry. That being said when you look under the covers there have been many disruptions that have affected me personally -

  • I was a little over a year in the job and the company created a new team to work with our largest Global System Integrator partners. I had done well in my first year and had been noticed as someone who could add value to this team and soon I was on a new team doing something completely different.
  • Fast forward a year and competition was heating up, our Q4 earnings weren't great and our stock dropped 50%. By the end of another year we had righted the ship but it also led to the company bringing in new leadership to take us to the next level.
  • New leadership meant new direction and more new leaders. The role I was in morphed into something else and I was off looking for a new challenge and soon moved over to manage our Customer Advocacy team.
  • Within a few months Tableau announced that we were transitioning our licensing model to Subscription. This led to the launch of a new Customer Success program and suddenly my team of 10 was going to be a team of 30 by the end of the year.
  • A few months ago Tableau announced that it was being bought by Salesforce.

These are just a few examples but it highlighted for me that just "doing my best" wasn't enough as the work environment around me was changing too fast and just because I was really good at what I was currently doing did not guarantee that it would be beneficial in what was coming around the bend.

Through this process I also started noticing that some old timers, people who had been very successful and built Tableau up were slowly moving out. The new talent that came in were finding solutions to challenges that had seemed impossible to the old guard.

  • Moving to subscription
  • Adding a top down selling motion to our traditional land and expand model
  • Changing our pricing to be more relevant and competitive.
  • Getting acquired

This journey has really opened my eye to the fact that we are constantly being disrupted in our jobs. Disruption is not out there, it is a reality we all face and to be successful one has to always stay ahead of the disruption. To do this, whenever I face a knotty challenge that in the past I might have said - "that's just how it is" or "that's too hard" etc. I now ask myself - "if I was let go and a new person came into my role, how would they solve this". This has been quite beneficial as it has helped me approach things from "first principles" or a "beginner's mind" and I find myself finding solutions that were in front of me but I was blind to. I am not saying I have figured it out as I still have a lot of blind spots and resort to "legacy thinking" - it's just easier and comfortable to not change.

Bottomline - "Doing my best" worked for a long time and that part of me will never change but in this age of disruption adding "staying ahead of disruption" has helped me grow in new ways and create the change.
