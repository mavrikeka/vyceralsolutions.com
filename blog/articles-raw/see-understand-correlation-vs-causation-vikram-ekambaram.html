See and Understand, Correlation vs Causation

Created on 2016-10-17 12:35

Published on 2016-10-17 13:48

I recently completed my 3 year anniversary at Tableau and obviously our mission statement (Help people see and understand data) is not something new to me. Similarly, I've known that correlation does not imply causation for a very long time. But something interesting happened last week. I stumbled onto a website - Spurious Correlations while I was preparing for a talk at a customer that incorporated our mission. These 2 random unconnected events gave me a whole new perspective to our mission.

There are lots of products that help people 'see' data. Charting tools, dashboarding tools, visualization tools. Often I get asked - "what is different about Tableau and why should I pay a premium for Tableau when I can get a cheaper / free tool that can build the same pretty pictures as Tableau". My response has always been that Tableau is a visual analytical tool that helps you go beyond the first set of questions that a chart or dashboard can answer. I've even blogged about this.

Which brings me to "correlation vs causation". You can 'see' a correlation but if you make decisions based on that correlation, you are going to be in big trouble. Decisions should be made based on the causation and finding the causation, requires you to 'understand' the data behind the correlation. It requires you to test out various hypothesis, collect additional data etc.

and a more serious take on this topic.

To me, it really comes down to that part of our mission that talks about 'see and understand'. It is not good enough to just 'see' data. You then you need to be able to 'understand' the data and to 'understand' the data you have to do further analysis, test out various hypotheses, apply context that only you as a business user will know, bring in additional data etc.

Does your data visualization tool let you do that deeper level of analytics or do you have to go off into Excel to understand what is really going on?
