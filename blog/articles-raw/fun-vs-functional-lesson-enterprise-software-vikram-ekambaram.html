FUN vs FUNctional - A lesson for Enterprise Software companies

Created on 2016-01-12 13:07

Published on 2016-01-12 13:47

We've all heard the saying 'practice makes perfect' or Malcolm Gladwell's 10,000 hour rule. Over the holidays I got a chance to observe my kids playing video games (whether on their digital devices or a game console). I had always thought of myself as a closet gamer. I have all the traits - an addictive personality, competitive nature and the ability to focus on a task for long amounts of time. So I tried my hand at a couple games and quickly realized that my kids were way better than me. Their fine motor skills, dexterity in handling the little controllers, make rapid movements with their fingers on a smartphone were off the chart.

Very quickly I came to the realization that I stood no chance. My only hope was to follow Malcolm Gladwell's advice and spend 10,000 hours on video games (luckily that was not an option). As I observe my kids and look at the effort they put in on other activities that us grown-ups want them to - homework, practicing piano, practicing tennis etc., it is obvious that these activities need a lot more 'pushing' from parents. So why is it that the same kids that are experts in video games and will spend hours on them, need constant pushing when it comes to other activities? It comes down to 'FUN'.

If you are having fun, you will love what you do and you will be self-motivated. You will spend 10,000 hours and you will be an expert. Obviously the gaming companies have taken FUN to a whole new level. I also see this with other 'Consumer' technologies- my mom who I will say is pretty digitally unsavvy and would not know how to navigate around a PC had no problem getting proficient on an iPad or WhatsApp. They were easy and fun and she gets better as she continues using them.

When I joined Tableau a couple years ago, I remembered hearing 2 words from my customers - Fun and Love. They kept telling me how much fun they were having and how much they loved Tableau / their job etc. Having worked in the enterprise software space for all my life these were 2 words I never heard customers use to describe the products I sold. For most people, the products they use at their work is WORK.. not FUN. It seemed that most other products elicited the same reaction that doing homework elicited from my kids, while Tableau was more like a video game.

Tableau was able to tap into something that brought out the kid inside us. Which probably explains why we had over 9000 customers at our customer conference last year. It also probably explains why we have grown from being a ~$40M company at the end of 2010 to a ~$400M company at the end of 2014, despite being in an extremely crowded and competitive marketplace.

With the workplace getting younger and the new generation having grown up on 'FUN' technologies, the challenge for Enterprise technology companies is going to be about creating technologies that are FUN to use and not just FUNctional.
