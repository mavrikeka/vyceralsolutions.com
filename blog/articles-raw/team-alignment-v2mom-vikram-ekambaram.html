Team Alignment - V2MOM

Created on 2020-04-10 14:09

Published on 2020-04-10 15:01

As a manager / leader, I've always prioritized alignment with my team. The team needs to know what our goals / priorities are, why these are important and how we are going to measure success. I spent a big chunk of my life in sales and this did not seem that important as my measure of success (making quota) was closely tied to making me making a living. That clear line between income and success, provided a clarity that did not make me wonder about what I should focus my time on.

The last few years, I've been leading customer success teams and the compensation is much more stable. So that straight line between my work and making money is blurred. The variable part of my compensation is based on a renewal number that for the most part is very stable. Working for a company with an awesome product and 90+% renewal rate means that my annual income is very stable and not as volatile as it was in Sales. That being said, it does raise many more existential questions. What is the impact I am having? How do I know if I am doing a good job? What does success look like in this role?

So as the leader of the team, I feel my biggest priority is to provide clarity around those questions. In the past, I've been good about explaining why and having a clear point of view on this topic when talking to the team. That being said, I would notice that I would get frustrated when the team did not 'get it' or were not executing to our priorities. I also would have folks say that when they heard me speak about it, it all made sense but then they got caught up in day to day execution. The other feedback was "you just added one more thing to the long list of things I am already doing. Yes, what you just added is important but I already have other priorities. So what do you want me to do?"

As part of being acquired by Salesforce, I got exposed to their V2MOM process for organizational alignment. It stands for Vision, Values, Methods, Obstacles, Measures. Since, my past approaches that usually centered around 'Start with why' didn't seem to completely hit the mark I decided to try out this new approach and it has been eye opening (at least for me :-)).

I'm managing a new team this year and we had some bandwidth and with COVID restricting travel, we decided to create our team V2MOM. To be fair when we started the process, we did not know we were going to create this but the feedback was loud and clear. The biggest priority for the team was "Clarity on what great looks like and what was expected of them. How would they know at the end of the year if they did a good job?"

So we did a daily stand up call for 2 weeks and jointly designed our V2MOM. The hardest part was Vision and Values. The framework I've used in the past have focused on Why, Success Criteria, Who, How, What. While Vision in some ways was similar to Why, Values were not something I had spent time on. As I spent time thinking about values, I realized that the times I got frustrated was when I felt there was a misalignment in mental models with my team. Clearly calling out the Vision and Values, helped us establish the mental model that we wanted to operate by. Btw. this is hard. Very hard. It forces you to really think about why and other existential questions, but when you have clarity of vision and values, magic happens.

So here is the V2 we came up with -

VISION

Our customers are expanding their partnership with Tableau because we are intentional about understanding their goals, planning their success and quarterbacking the resources needed to drive business outcomes. We are supporting their transformation in becoming data driven organizations. We are a team of critical thinkers that are defining the customer success craft.

VALUES

Outcome driven - We will be intentional about our activities delivering outcomes. No checking the check box.

Empathy - Change is hard, so we will approach our work with empathy while realizing that we still need to drive business outcomes.

Strategic - We will deliver business outcomes (internal and external) by first understanding why (what problem we are trying to solve), what outcomes we are trying to drive (what success looks like) and what the decision making process is (who cares enough to do something about it).

Bias for Action - With the rapid pace of change around us, we have to forge new paths and with urgency. We have to be vigilant about trying new things, intentional about learning from our efforts, applying the learning and trying again.

Player coach - We will own delivering business outcomes while scaling our efforts by teaching customers how to take ownership of their success and leveraging our extended team.

I won't share the details around the MOM (Methods, Obstacles, Measures) but now that everyone was clear on our Vision and Values, we could clearly call out 'What' we wanted to do to achieve the vision (Methods), what would stop us from achieving those (Obstacles) and what our goals were (Measures). We added an S to the V2MOM, which were Solutions to the Obstacles as it made sense for us.

The V2MOM is not a static document and I am sure as the year progresses we will find out we missed some stuff but the beauty of having it all written down is that the team has clarity on what is expected. If they are asked to do something, then they can do what our CEO Marc Benioff always says - "if it is not in your V2MOM don't do it". Which then forces leadership to re-evaluate our priorities and either modify the V2MOM or not add one more thing to the list.

The other side effect has been that the team would get questions from their extended team (Account Execs etc.) about 'what do you do' / 'what can I expect from you' and now they can just share their V2MOM document and get feedback from the extended team on their expectations and we can expand the V2MOM.

Curious how others do this and what approach they use.
