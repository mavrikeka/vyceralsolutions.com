Tableau Blueprint - Getting strategic

Created on 2020-03-09 01:18

Published on 2020-03-09 02:45

Tableau launched Blueprint, a methodology to help customers scale Tableau across the enterprise, last year. What is great about Blueprint is that it focuses not just on the technology but also on the 'people and process' aspects required to be successful in scaling Tableau.

The reason Tableau created Blueprint was because Self Service analytics brings with it a unique challenge that most organizations are not prepared for. Not since Excel has there been a technology that is so ubiquitous that any any knowledge worker can use it. You can be in Finance, HR, Marketing, Operations etc. and you can all benefit from self service analytics. In fact organizations are working hard to create a data culture as they see the benefits of business users asking questions of their own data. Each of these knowledge workers is not just consuming analytics (dashboard or reports) but also doing their own analysis from scratch.

To be successful with self-service analytics requires new roles, new teams and a new approach to analytics. So the first 2 steps of the Blueprint start with Analytics Strategy and Executives and Teams.


That being said, when we ask customers what their Analytics Strategy is or how are they organized to scale self service analytics across their enterprise, we are often met with puzzled looks. So we end up pivoting to the elements on the right of the Blueprint that we can start executing on.

That being said, while we get some tactical wins, by not focusing on the first 2 elements we lose the opportunity to have the bigger conversation around data culture and organization change. What we also end up with is customers pushing back because it is 'too hard' / 'too expensive' / 'takes too much time' / 'needs too many people'. Fact is no one said creating a data culture was going to be easy. In fact HBR did a survey on how organizations are struggling with this. IMHO, the reason they struggle is because they don't know WHY they are doing this, they don't know how to MEASURE the business impact and they don't know how they should organize and WHO should do what.

The intent of this post is to leverage a modified Simon Sinek, start with the why, to help have the strategic conversations with the customer. So rather than asking 'What is your Analytics Strategy' and being met with puzzled looks, how can we ask the right questions to get customers to really spend some time thinking about the first 2 steps of the Blueprint.

The questions I ask are -

  1. Why did you make this investment? (or a variation.. why did you not buy half the licenses?)
  2. How will you Measure success of this investment? (or.. What is the business value you are looking to create?)
  3. Who cares about the success of this investment? (or.. who cares about creating a data culture / scaling out analytics successfully?)

It would be great if just asking the above 3 questions would help us with the getting all our answers for the first 2 steps of the Blueprint. Obviously, it is not that easy. The key is to keep digging till we get a good answer to the above questions. Here is where it can go sideways.

  • Chances are we might be talking to the wrong people, who are far removed from the actual funding decision.
  • The other chance is that it was not a strategic decision. They were not looking to scale self service analytics across the organization and it was just a tactical purchase.

Both of the above are great outcomes as it should make us pause and really think about how we proceed. If we are talking to the wrong people, how do we get to the right people? If they don't care about scaling self service analytics, why talk to them about Blueprint? Why not just treat it as a tactical engagement? In parallel, try to find someone who cares about analytics as a strategic investment.

Let's say we get an answer along the lines of "analytics is key and we see the value of rolling out analytics to all our users". The next question is - how are you planning to measure if you will be successful with rolling out analytics? This will cause people to really stop and think as very few organizations have thought about the business impact of self service analytics. They understand the impact of analytics, but what is the value of having a knowledge worked in Supply Chain/Sales/Real Estate asking questions of her own data? This is the point, where we will have to start getting to the business groups and business executives. Just working with the central teams (IT / Procurement / COE etc.) will not give us the true level of impact. Only the business groups can quantify the impact to their business.

Once we have interviewed the main business groups and business executives we will have an idea of what kind of impact the organization is looking to have with analytics. This will give us the starting point to create value from the investment. We will have visibility into key use cases / projects. This will take work as analytics is truly ubiquitous and we have to get to lots of business groups and executives.

At this point in the process, we now know 'WHY' this is important to them and how they will 'MEASURE" the success of self-service analytics. The next step is 'WHO'. Who are the executives who care about this? Who are the teams they have in place to scale out self service analytics? This is also the step where customers realize that they are understaffed. They haven't thought about how they are planning to train and enable thousands of knowledge workers. They haven't thought about how business groups need to train their knowledge workers on data sources and key dashboards. They haven't thought about how they will keep this community of users engaged and supported. They haven't thought about who is responsible for defining the governance that underpins any successful self service technology.

That being said, if we did not spend the time upfront clearly defining 'WHY' this was important and 'MEASURE' the business impact, then we hit the standard roadblocks - this will take too much time, too many resources, will be too expensive. Good news is that if we know the why and the business impact, we can make the case to fund the right teams, get the right executive sponsorship which are both going to be key to long term success.

Now that we have done the WHY, MEASURE and WHO we can get to the HOW and the WHAT. HOW do we scale out analytics - by setting up an agile environment, with the right governance, rolling out analytics proficiency and creating a thriving user community. The HOW are the elements on the right side of the Blueprint.

Lastly then we get to the WHAT is needed for each of the above areas? We have all the details in the Blueprint.

As one can see, we have to stop ourselves from jumping into the HOW and the WHAT. Start with WHY, then MEASURE, then WHO and then the HOW and WHAT. If we can follow the above approach we can really help organizations scale analytics across the enterprise and create a data culture. If we cannot, start with WHY, MEASURE and WHO, then maybe they are not ready to scale out analytics and that is totally fine. Start with the HOW and the WHAT but be intentional about using the HOW and the WHAT to build the trust / engagement to pivot to the WHY, MEASURE and WHO.
