I object to Objection Handling

Created on 2016-02-19 21:42

Published on 2016-02-19 22:26

Working for an enterprise software company, one tends to get very good at objection handling. There is training, content and a lot of material that is internally created to handle objections. There are frameworks on how to handle objections (one of these is in the header for this post). While these are important and good to understand, there are cases where objection handling doesn't work.

The other day, I was in a meeting and got to experience objection handling as the customer. This role reversal was quite insightful. The person I was talking to was telling me what to do and I found myself coming up with all the reasons I did not want to follow along. I had a long list of objections and every time there was a solution to the objection, I found myself coming up with another objection. I was doing a great job of playing devil's advocate. The bottom line was that I did not believe in what they were saying.

As you can imagine the conversation was going nowhere, until the person stopped responding to my objections and stopped telling me what to do. Instead he turned it around and explained the situation to me. Then he asked me what I would do. Suddenly, I could not play devil's advocate anymore and I found myself coming up with solutions.

My learning from this was that there are situations where you need to provide the context (get to the crux of the matter) and then ask the question "What would you do?".

For example, at Tableau we always push the customers to do a "Guided Evaluation" - where we get the end users hands on with the product with their data. I sometimes get push back from customers and I find myself responding to their objections. The next time I  am going to get to the crux of the matter - "We have an opportunity to enable the people who know the data, to ask their own questions?" Then ask the "What would you do" question - How would you structure an evaluation that would allow the end users to try before they buy? Curious how this will work out. I can think of many competitive situations where I can apply this.

One of the best salesperson I worked with would always tell me that he spent more time 'disqualifying a deal' rather than 'qualifying a deal'. He would have the customer explain why they wanted to do an evaluation, rather than spend time convincing the customer why they should evaluate his product - and it worked for him.
