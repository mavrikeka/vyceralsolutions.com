Smarter than a 7th grader? A lesson for organizations

Created on 2015-12-28 03:33

Published on 2015-12-28 04:05

When I was growing up and in 7th grade, I am pretty sure that my dad was better than me in almost everything. Especially anything that involved strategy and knowledge. It was a different world back then and age meant experience and intelligence.

Over the last few weeks I have been home a bit more and have had a chance to observe not just my 7th grader but also my 7 year old and I've come to the conclusion that they are better than me in quite a few areas. The internet has evened out the 'knowledge gap' as they can access information as quickly as I can. The fact that I 'know' something doesn't mean much as they can access that information at their fingertips.

What really surprised me was how online games have also evened out the playing field when it comes to strategy. Whether it is Pokemon or Clash of Clans, they are definitely ahead of me. I am on a clan where I am being taught strategy by other kids (teenagers and younger). I find myself in a weird situation where I ask for ideas and advice on strategies from kids. In this virtual world we are in, age is trumped by knowledge. These kids have gained knowledge by 'doing'. They didn't start by reading a manual, they started playing and collaboratively by talking to their friends, got good. If I want to learn about strategies around Clash of Clans, I am reading blogs and watching videos created by kids. They definitely understand resource allocation, resource management, trade-offs, collaboration, strategy etc.

I wonder how these kids feel when they graduate from college and show up for work. They are given technologies that were written before they were born, they are treated like they don't know anything and they are placed in an environment where age and experience is the criteria for success / contribution. Are we empowering the new generation to maximize their potential? Are we giving them an opportunity to be the best they can be?

It reminds me of a Tableau visualization contest we ran at one of our large customers. We gave out an AirBnB dataset to about 400 employees and asked them to present a dashboard with an analysis of the data. We got 40 entries. The entries were from experienced data analysts to interns, senior folks and junior folks, people who had been long time Tableau users and new Tableau users. When the results were announced we were surprised to find that the top 2 entries were from recent college grads who had been at the company less than 2 years and had less than 6 months experience with Tableau.

Are organizations applying old rules to a new world? How do you keep this new generation motivated?
