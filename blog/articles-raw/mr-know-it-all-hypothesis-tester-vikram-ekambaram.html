Mr. Know-It-All to Hypothesis Tester

Created on 2015-12-17 05:10

Published on 2015-12-17 05:44

The world has changed a lot since I went to school. It used to be that being a straight A student meant something. You got great grades if you knew all the answers. Preparation was key. This carried on for me to my career. When I was in pre-sales, I prided myself on knowing the answers to a lot of the questions my customers had. Somewhere along the way, I also realized that 'playing safe' was key. Why take a risk and look bad.

Last year, I did a Storyleaders training with some of my colleagues. One of the concepts covered was 'vulnerability'. There was a great Ted Talk from Brene Brown. Coming from India, being a guy etc. this was new to me. While the concept was intriguing, I was not ready to embrace it. I was happy being Mr Know-It-All, being the expert. There was a power in knowing a lot of the answers or being able to quickly find one if I didn't know it.

This last year, I've had to step outside my 'safe zone' and try new things. Somewhere along the way I became a 'Hypothesis Tester'. I got comfortable saying - "I don't know if this will work but let's try it out". I got comfortable doing things even if I wasn't sure if it would work out. If it didn't work out, it was a learning experience and not a failure. I still relapse into Mr Know-It-All mode often but it has been a freeing experience to be willing to try out hypothesis and fail fast.

I remember my stint as a manager, when I prided myself in putting on my super-hero costume and swooping in to save the day. Failure was not an option. My Sales VP loved me as he could count on me to deliver results and if something went wrong I would step in. Back then, I assumed my team was pretty happy with me as I was willing to step in and roll up my sleeves to help out as needed (at least that is what I told myself). Now I think about those days and realize that - yes we didn't let anything drop but maybe I took away some great learning opportunities for my team.

As the world around us changes quickly and we have to constantly figure out new ways of doing things, I think the ability to be more 'Hypothesis Tester' than 'Mr Know-It-All', is going to be a very useful skill. Somewhere along the way, I learnt to be a little 'vulnerable'.
