Software Evaluation - Do RFPs / POCs really work?

Created on 2016-04-28 12:53

Published on 2016-04-28 13:40

Software evaluation is a very interesting process that usually starts out with an RFI/RFP, moves on to a POC (proof of concept) and ends up with a selection / negotiation. Often the customer has a pre-disposition even before the process has started and this whole process is really a justification of that pre-disposed intention.

Is this really the best way to evaluate software? Often a software purchase once made will last you years, and the wrong decision can have significant long term impact. Couple that with the fact that the person who makes the decision, will do everything (spend significant money on consulting services / customization) even when they realize that they are throwing good money after bad.

The question I ask - is there a better way? Why do companies do RFPs / POCs? Why don't they use the software before they buy? Why is there surprise when projects fail or customers end up with shelfware?

When I think about some of the big purchase decisions we make in life - buying a car, buying a house, I am reminded of how the process is inadequate. Buying a house is a lot like buying software. One tends to do a walk through, look at the features, pay for an inspection etc. You then buy the house and move in. A few months later it rains really hard and you realize that the basement tends to get water seepage on heavy rainy days or that a pipe tends to freeze in really cold nights in the winter unless there is heat in the basement etc. Again, there would be no way to know this unless one lived in the house before they bought it.

I remember test driving a car (drove around for 30 mins) and it felt great. A week later I realized that if I drove the car for a couple hours every day, the way the seat was structured my back would hurt. But now I was stuck with the car. There was no way for me to find this out in a test drive.

What if you could take your car home for a month and use it in your day to day life before you ended up buying the car? What if you could live in your house for a year before you had to buy your house. It seems the right thing to do to be as certain as possible (no guarantees in life) but it also seems absurd.

Coming back to software evaluation - what if you could actually use the software before you bought it? Imagine you are evaluating a self-service analytics technology. The intent is that your business end users will be using the product to analyze their own data. The current process is - IT led RFP that evaluates the products on feature / functions (everyone has the same features), followed by a demo that the vendor does (everyone looks the same), then a POC that the vendor does with your data (all the dashboards built look the same) and then a selection based on price because they all look the same. Then you give the technology to the end users and they say it is too complicated and they go back to Excel and you wonder where you went wrong.

Imagine if we replaced this with, handing the software over to the business users to do their own analysis with their own data. Try it out for a week, ask your real questions, play around with the technology so you understand what it can and cannot do. Prepare for the evaluation by thinking about the more complicated analysis you tend to do. See if the product can handle that. At the end of the week, you have real analysis you have done, by yourself, with your data and you can actually use the analysis to make decisions. When the purchase is finally made, there are no surprises. You don't end up with the - I thought it could do this because I saw it in the demo, I thought this was easier but now that I try this myself, it is very complicated.

I am in a conference where the theme is "Zero-Distance". Zero-Distance is key to innovation. Maybe it is time to have Zero Distance in software evaluation. Learn by doing not observing.
