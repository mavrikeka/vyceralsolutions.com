Grassroots Innovation and the Iceberg of Ignorance

Created on 2016-05-06 12:58

Published on 2016-05-06 13:33

I was at a conference recently and one of the points made was that innovation is not the role of a select group of individuals within an organization but the responsibility of everyone. The term used was 'Grassroots Innovation' and this hit home for me as I feel strongly that any individual in an organization can do amazing things, given the right tools and support. I'd even blogged about 'Unleashing Human Potential' back in Dec. I also have personal experience with unleashing my own potential as I've discovered a whole new side of me (as an active blogger) that I did not know existed.

Yesterday a colleague of mine pointed me to a study done back in 1989 called the Iceberg of Ignorance by Sidney Yoshida and here is an image that shows this (by Bobby Albert)

Without getting into the specific percentages, what it says is that Top Managers only know a small percentage of the problems that an organization faces, while the front-line employees tends to know 100% of the problems.

When you look at it this way, isn't it obvious that innovation will happen at all levels? Should we not be enabling the front line employees with the right tools and data, so they can find insights that will help them innovate? Aren't front line employees the first to see the effects of disruption and thus the first that can react to that disruption? Aren't front line employees also least likely to hold on to old ways of doing things, especially when they realize it is not working anymore?

The side effect of Grassroots Innovation, is that employees will be more engaged and motivated as they will realize that they are making a difference. Now, I am not saying that innovation should only happen at the front-line. Front-line employees tend to have a narrow perspective of the world and often it helps to take a step back and look at the problem holistically and from multiple perspectives.

BTW, has anyone noticed how the 'Iceberg' has become the visualization of choice to depict the 80-20 rule?
