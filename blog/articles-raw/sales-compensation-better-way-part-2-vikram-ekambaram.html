Sales Compensation, better way, Part 2

Created on 2015-11-11 06:01

Published on 2015-11-11 06:45

My earlier post on sales compensations need to be disrupted got me quite a few interesting responses, that it got me to write this follow on post. An old friend of mine called me and asked me if I was planning to become the Jerry Maguire of Sales. I knew what he meant (if there was going to be a manifesto somewhere along the way) although it is ironic that the main line everyone remembers from the movie is "Show me the money".

The question that has been going around in my mind is whether sales compensation plans will get disrupted. I wanted to see if the past could be a good indicator of the future and I would like to share some examples that give me hope.

I remember the first time I sat in a QBR (Quarterly Business Review) as a presales engineer back in early 2000. The Sales VP did what I had heard every Sales VP does. He ripped into the under-performing reps. There was no constructive criticism or coaching, it was pure humiliation in front of his peers. Pipeline review calls were the same. I am glad to say that those days seem long gone. Most QBRs now have managers trying to understand how they can work with the rep to get to the results. It is much more consultative and you get the feeling that people are working towards success rather than pointing fingers.

I remember when sales reps were hired based on their rolodex. Relationship selling was how it was done. Somewhere along the way that changed and companies wanted more of a 'Challenger' sales person. Someone that was not just going to be the customer's friend and not ask for the business but someone that was willing to ask the tough questions while building relationships based on trust and value.

I remember when Art of War and Hunting etc. were synonymous with sales. It was about identifying pain and asking questions that led to pain. Conjured up images of 2 sides going to battle. That has now changed to being a 'Storyleader'. Where it is OK to not have all the answers and show some vulnerability.

I remember when it was all about ROIs. Till people realized that the ROIs were never delivered and that got replaced with Value Selling.

Even the technology has changed. I remember when you used Siebel and then there was this upstart Salesforce that wanted people to put their data in the Cloud.

With this backdrop of constant change towards something that is more meaningful and real, I think there is hope for disruption. As another friend commented - Commissions strip the dignity of sales people and sadly I cannot disagree.

I feel lucky that I've had a manager who cared about doing right by the customer, knowing that the money would follow and even if the money did not follow you felt fulfilled. I also feel lucky that I sell a product where I can look a customer in the eye and say - rather than doing a big deal let's start small, deliver value, then grow, deliver more value. I could never do this if the product didn't work or if it required a large amount of consulting to get off the ground.
