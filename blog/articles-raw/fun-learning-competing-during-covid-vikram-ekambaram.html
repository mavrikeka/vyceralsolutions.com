Fun, Learning and Competing during COVID

Created on 2021-04-11 16:19

Published on 2021-04-11 17:13

I have written in the past about Destination Imagination (DI), the not for profit that is teaching kids about creative collaborative problem solving. I have been lucky to be a team manager and have a ringside view to how this process works in young kids. Like every other organization DI has had to pivot and adapt to the new normal we live in and this year's competition was a virtual affair. Which meant socially distanced meetings for the kids, a virtual competition where you had to submit your entry via video and a competition where you could not be in the same physical space as other teams

I was not too sure how this year would be. The kids were 'zoomd' out and the thought of getting 5 7th graders motivated to work together virtually and during the winter was a concern. But like everything else with this pandemic, I was very very surprised at how resilient the kids are and how they find opportunity in every challenge. I am always reminded about how much I learn every time I team manage and this was one of many aha's that the kids taught me.

So the challenge that the kids set out to solve was - Create and present a video presentation in the style of a video game. Include an adventurer(s) that goes on a quest and wins an ultimate prize.

I am so proud of their entry that won 1st place in their category and won a 'Renaissance Award' and now they get to compete at Global Finals. While the end result was impressive, what I got to see was the learning and the journey..

The word 'pivot' is used a lot nowadays in the business world and I got to see this in action with the kids. Once they realized it was virtual they started brainstorming various virtual presentations - video games, movies, short films, animation, virtual reality, go-pro live action, drone footage were all discussed. They individually then researched each as a viable solution - testing out an animation software, recording using an iPhone using a greenscreen, building a scratch video game, using minecraft etc. They discussed as a team and guess what - the final solution like every other innovation had a bit of all of the solutions. At work we talk about 'fall in love with the problem and not the solution' and I was seeing this in action. The kids were learning Design Thinking principles and not the theory or an online course. This was true practical usage.

So what all did they learn over the last 4 months? Firstly they learnt how collaborative projects are done. You know, the stuff no one teaches you - the first 3 months you meet for a couple hours every week but the last couple weeks are crunch time and you spend a lot of time. For example, they thought the final editing would take a couple hours - it took a full day.

In terms of the skills learnt -

  1. How to build an animation - Krita
  2. How to compose music - Soundtrap.
  3. How to shoot using a greenscreen with an iPhone
  4. How to use iMovie to edit it all together.
  5. How to build a contraption to combine liquids (Rube Goldberg).. btw there is no YouTube video that shows using a Rube Goldberg machine to automatically pour and combine liquids.
  6. How a chemical reaction of yeast, water, soap and hydrogen peroxide creates 'Elephant's toothpaste'.
  7. How to build a magnetic mixer. btw, this involved taking a fan out of a computer and the magnet out of a hard drive.
  8. How to power up the magnetic mixer.
  9. Write a story, create props, costumes.
  10. The most important learning - you have to have FUN!!!

Through the process they figured out how to divide up the work so each person could work independently but collaborate to make sure that they were all aligned on the end goal. What was most impressive was that, they had no knowledge of how to do any of these things before this project. I remember having my doubts and this was another learning - when a group's creativity is sparked the impossible becomes possible and as the team manager all I had to do was get out of the way. Lastly, none of this would be possible without the support of the parents.
