Developing Curiosity - How I got lost and learnt to walk

Created on 2016-06-23 15:38

Published on 2016-06-24 14:22

I've written a few posts on the topic of curiosity, although I don't consider myself a naturally curious person. We all know someone that is naturally curious, they are like a kid in a candy store, constantly learning / questioning, seeing things we don't and experiencing life to the fullest. My dad was like that.

But what if you are like me - going through life in auto-pilot mode, lost in your thoughts? We all know the feeling, you are driving in your car lost in thought and you realize that you have been driving for 5 mins with no conscious awareness of the traffic, but anytime the car in front of you braked you saw the brake lights and snapped to attention. Or you are glued to your smartphone and don't notice your surrounding.

I have bad shin splints. For those of you who have shin splints, you know the symptoms - you walk uphill or walk fast and your shin muscles inflame and you can't walk anymore. You have to walk really slowly while your shins burn. The solution for me is to put my legs in a bucket of ice after I get home. Yes I do this a lot.

A couple weeks ago, I decided to consciously turn on my awareness and observe what was causing the shin splints and I noticed that I tend to walk in a flat footed way, my ankles tend to feel locked. It is almost like I lift my foot rather than let it roll from heel to the ball of my feet. It is hard to explain. Anyway, I decided to try an experiment and exaggerate the lifting of my heel every time I had to take my foot off the ground. This engaged the calf muscles and took a lot of pressure off my shins. My shins still hurt but I could now go for longer walks without the debilitating pain.

The second part of this post is about getting lost. I take a regular walk from my house, through the neighborhood to a park and then to an arboretum. It is a 1 hour loop and I tend to do the walk in auto-pilot mode. I put on my headset, listen to music and do the same loop every time. There are side trails in the arboretum, but I've never taken them. I was never curious, it just seemed like a lot of work to try a new path, plus I had a schedule to keep.

The other day, I was listening to Stairway to Heaven just before I got to the arboretum and for some reason, these words stood out -

"Yes, there are two paths you can go by, But in the long run There's still time to change the road you're on"

Now I've listened to Stairway a thousand times and these words never seemed special. Anyway, when I got to the arboretum I decided to take one of the side trails (power of suggestion?) and I realized it would take me longer to get back but I knew worst case I could double back (but in the long run there's still time to change the road you are on).

Long story short I got lost. I thought I knew where the new trail would take me but one trail led to another and soon I was deep in the woods. The trails were marked and I knew eventually I'd find a path that would lead me out (so I wasn't worried). Plus in today's world of cell phones, one is never truly lost.

Something interesting happened once I got lost. I got out of auto-pilot mode. Suddenly I was very aware of where I was walking, I paid attention to details that I would normally not pay attention to. I also became hyper aware of my shins as I realized it was going to be a long walk. I noticed that my new walking style definitely allowed me to walk much faster (and I needed to walk faster as I was lost). I also realized that walking on dirt put much less pressure on my shins. Two hours later I was back home but I felt different - I felt pumped up because I had tried something new (faced the fear of the unknown?), I had figured something out about my walking style and found some new trails (learning something new is fun) and I realized that sometimes the fun is in the journey and not the destination.

So the next time you feel stuck - you are grappling with an issue, you are trying to solve a problem, doing some analysis on a data set that is not going anywhere etc. - go and get lost. It will turn on a different part of your brain and you will probably find an out of the box solution. Always remember -

But in the long run There's still time to change the road you're on.
