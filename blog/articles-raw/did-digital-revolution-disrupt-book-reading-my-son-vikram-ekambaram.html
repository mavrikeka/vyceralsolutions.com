Did the digital revolution disrupt book reading for my son?

Created on 2015-10-22 22:34

Published on 2015-10-22 23:13

My son does not like reading. Actually if I ask him he'll say he hates reading. Not sure how he can have such strong emotions at such a young age. Although I do think he is exaggerating. Given a choice he would not read. This is something alien to my wife and I, as we are both avid readers.

So like all good parents who KNOW what is best for their kids we do the righteous thing of constantly telling him that he should read. As you can imagine this has not been a successful strategy. He does read a little but it feels like he does that under duress or when he has to do it for school.

Every once in a while a thought crosses my mind - Why is reading a book so important? This is an interesting question but sounds like sacrilege. Let's start with what I think is the main reason -

1. LEARNING - When I was growing up I did not travel a lot (didn't leave the country till I was 25). I lived in a small town in India and most places I visited were in my imagination. Most people I met were Indian. Most foods I ate were Indian. Books were great as we did not have TV or videos (that came much later - 9th grade ish). My son has traveled to more countries in his short life and seen more places, experienced more cultures that reading about them is not that invigorating. As far as learning goes, he learns stuff on the internet, he solves puzzles in minecraft, plays strategy games in Pokemon. He lives in NYC and experiences various cultures first hand. He has a smartphone where he can find the answer to any question in 15 seconds. So making the argument that reading helps him learn is starting to sound weak.

 2. ENTERTAINMENT - I can remember the hours of entertainment I got from reading. I still do. My son is entertained by various digital media (video games, TV, YouTube etc.). Also, TV shows and documentaries are great at educating since there are more entertaining and easier to remember. I get it that reading a book is a much richer experience, that a shallow movie or TV show cannot replace the characters that come to life in one's imagination but how can that compete against multimedia?

3. FRIEND REPLACEMENT - I had friends when I was growing up but to go meet them in person required effort. You could talk on the telephone but that was mostly to schedule a meet up, not to talk. When I did not feel like making the effort to meet my friends, I would read a book. My son is in constant touch with his friends via various Social Media outlets. He never feels friend deprived (although he often chooses to be friend deprived and get digitally drugged).

4. FAMILY - When I was growing up everyone in my family read (and they read a lot). You would never see my mom without a book. My dad was reading books and magazines and newspapers. The print media was a constant presence. It seemed normal to read. What does my son see at home - his dad is on the computer all day (working!!), no one reads newspapers or magazines at home, most books are read on a Kindle. If someone has a question, we don't go to the library, we google it.

This then brought me to look at my life today and what is it that I read that is a book (even if it is digital). Most of the books I read are around self-improvement and things that are related to work. Mostly non-fiction. Every once in a while I will read some fiction. In some ways, my reading habits have been disrupted too.

So is it a losing battle we fight?
