Coaching vs. Mentoring

Created on 2019-05-15 15:32

Published on 2019-05-17 16:37

A few years ago I had written a post about the difference between being a 'Problem Solver' vs a 'Solution Enabler'. This is something that I have struggled with as I cannot turn my brain off. Any time someone brings up something, my brain goes into problem solving mode. It is just how I am wired (or so I tell myself). That being said, I have learnt over the years that invariably any solution I come up with is often 'ok' but not the 'best' or 'right' solution. I am also very aware that any solution is one of many many possibilities.

I got a first hand reminder of this the other day working with my 10 year old and his friend in building structures for a science fair. We did our research and realized that structures with triangles were stronger than structures with squares.

We were also trying to create the lightest structure that could hold the most weight. I thought having a structure with lots of triangles would be much more stable than one with fewer triangles. So I built one with lots of triangles (took forever) and my son his friend built one with few triangles (much less time and effort). When we actually got around to testing, my structure had a lower weight held ratio (weight held / weight of structure). This was a great reminder that while I might have an opinion that my idea is better (and we were all convinced, including my son that the structure I built would have the better ratio) , reality is often different. Which is why coaching is so important. It lets each person find their own path. If I had mentored them, I'd have had them build my idea of the 'best' structure.


On the left is the structure I built and on the right the structure my son and his friend built.



Here is the definition of coaching vs mentoring - "Mentors can be more ‘directive’ and provide specific advice where appropriate - a coach would not offer their own advice or opinion, but help the individual find their own solution. A coach will assist, challenge and encourage rather than direct, advise or teach"

So the learning I've had with coaching -

  1. If the person I am coaching does not have a strong opinion and I am trying to 'assist, challenge, encourage', it often ends up like I am leading the witness.
  2. Similarly, if I am knowledgeable about the topic I am coaching, it is really hard for me to 'not offer my own advice or opinion'.
  3. I've found that cases where I am not 'vested' or an expert are the cases where I can be the best coach as I am listening and since I don't know the answers, I can be curious without having an opinion.

So the big question - in a work situation, where often as a manager you are knowledgeable about the business, how can a problem solver truly coach?
