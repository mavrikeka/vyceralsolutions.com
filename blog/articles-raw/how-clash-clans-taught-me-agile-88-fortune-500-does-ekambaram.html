How Clash of Clans taught me about Agile - 88% of the Fortune 500 does not exist

Created on 2015-12-11 12:35

Published on 2015-12-11 13:12

I am a Sales guy and sell a data visualization product. There should be no good reason for me to be talking about Agile (isn't that what developers do), or so you would think.

My son and I have been playing Clash of Clans and for those of you who don't know what that is - it is a mobile multiplayer strategy game where you build communities, defenses etc. You get attacked and you can attack others.

Each time you get attacked you get some ideas on what defenses work, what your defensive strategy should be etc. You start off with a particular defense since you don't know if it will work, then you get attacked, you get feedback, you tweak the defense to better protect yourself, then someone stronger beats you and you tweak it again. Every attack is feedback to improve your defenses. You are in this constant loop of - research, come up with a hypothesis, prototype, iterate. Every loss is not a mistake, but an opportunity to learn and get better. I'm also collaborating with my son and learning from him and my 'clan'. The 'clan' is a group of online players who get together and share resources, ideas and strategies.

As you can see this is exactly how Agile works and funnily enough, how Tableau customers develop dashboards. You have some questions, you get with your data person, you create a hypothesis, you get in a room and jointly build a prototype, as you build the prototype you come up with more questions that you add to the dashboard and then you finalize and launch it. You get feedback and you iterate. So the dashboard is never really done, it keeps evolving. It is not about spending a month creating requirements documents that no one reads and that are outdated by the time you end up building the dashboard. It's about learning through the journey and not focusing on the destination.

My Aha moment was in realizing that building a dashboard in a lot of ways is like planning my defenses. You don't really know what will work. You have a hypothesis and the only real way to know if it will work is to build a prototype (learn while building because once you have the prototype you can see where the weaknesses are), then launch it, see what works and then tweak it. The collaborative piece is also critical as you have multiple perspectives on how to approach the dashboard / defense.

Lately, I have been winning a lot of my defenses (at my level) and have been feeling pretty good. I was patting myself on the back about how 'I've got this'. Then I saw an attack today that I had never expected. It was a balloon / healing spell combo (I won't get into details) that totally decimated the defense. It wasn't something I couldn't do, I just never thought about it. Blew my mind, my ego and defenses.

We live in a world of so much change and disruption. Here is an interesting statistic - 88% of the Fortune 500 from 1955 no longer exists. These were our best and biggest and most successful companies. In a world with so much disruption, the old ways of coming up with 5 year plans, strategies and mandates driven from top-down only, CEO and his management team know best etc. don't really define success. The days of spending months coming up with a strategy don't bode well for you because by the time the strategy is done the world has changed. You need to be agile, you need to realize that you don't have all the answers (and you will not have all the answers, so don't spend all your time trying to find them), you need to be willing to cultivate a culture of risk taking / failing fast and you need to empower everyone in your organization as you don't know where the next best idea will come from.

Lastly, anytime you tell yourself - "I got this", just remember that someone is going to come from left field and disrupt you. I am glad that my 7 year old is learning about agile / experimentation / trying things out. It's a skill that is going to be very useful.
