Self-Service does not mean Self-Enablement - Another Clash of Clans example

Created on 2016-03-14 13:03

Published on 2016-03-14 13:44

Some of the biggest trends in technology - cloud, mobile, social, analytics etc. have all led to empowering the end user. This trend towards disintermediation where the producer and the consumer can now connect directly has been accelerating due to the internet and technology - all you need is an app. We see this with Uber, Amazon, eBay, Facebook etc.

In the world of Analytics, we've seen the same trend with technologies that allow business users to visually analyze their data. But, when it comes to enabling the business users to learn the technologies, enterprises seem to be following the precedence set by consumer technologies. If someone can figure out how to use an iPad or Facebook or play a game by themselves, they can surely learn how to use the self-service analytics tool. If they get stuck, they can always google and find the answer.

Sadly, all this does is lead to spotty adoption of the technology. In case you are thinking that the reason for spotty adoption is due to the analytics products being too complicated and the onus is on the software vendor to create something as easy as Facebook, I'd like to take the example of Clash of Clans and my experience in learning to play the game.

Clash of Clans is a mobile online multiplayer strategy game and I started playing it 6 months ago and I've learnt a lot (not just about the game). The rules are pretty straight-forward and anyone can learn the basics with minimal effort (as you would expect from a game). Within the first couple weeks you can be playing and having fun. At some point you start realizing that there are others who are much better at it than you. Their worlds are more sophisticated and their attacks are more sophisticated. You also realize that you are not going to get better just by playing. How many of us really use all the features in Word or Excel?

I got to the 'next' level by joining a clan. A community of players who helped get my skills to the next level by providing me tips and by me watching their attacks and defenses. Even this was not that straightforward as the first clan I joined was not that great. They really weren't helping each other and I didn't learn much. The second clan I joined was engaged and committed and I saw my skills skyrocket. I got lucky. If the second clan was also so-so, I might have quit the game.

Now I am at the stage where I need to learn from other sources (Youtube / blogs etc.) as even my clan has certain blind spots. Certain strategies work better and over time we all start using those strategies and forget that there are others available. The learning for me has been that even with something as consumer friendly as an online game, if you want people to get better and stay engaged you need to provide a medium for enablement. In the Clash of Clans ecosystem, if some players are good and others are bad, it does not mean much. It does not really impact the game.

Bringing the conversation back to business users learning self-service analytics tools, if organizations want the most value from their investments in people and technology, they need to take enablement seriously. Unlike Clash of Clans, establishing an analytical culture can add tremendous value to an organization (especially in today's environment of disruption and data) . Spotty adoption leads to lost opportunity.

Organizations need to establish a strong community that can raise everyone's skills, they need to have content (blogs / best practices etc.) available through intranets, they need to have experts available to help people when they are stuck, they need to provide formal training. In short they need to invest in enablement.

Sadly, most organizations treat enablement as a part time job with no funding - where someone in their free time is learning the technology, someone in their free time is managing the intranet, someone in their free time is conducting lunch and learns. Organizations are getting value from self-service analytics but to drive true innovation and create a culture of analytics, enablement has to be funded.
