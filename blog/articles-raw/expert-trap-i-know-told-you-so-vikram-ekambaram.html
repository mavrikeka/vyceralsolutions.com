The Expert Trap - I know it, I told you so

Created on 2016-01-17 16:43

Published on 2016-01-20 02:18

A lot of us have heard about the 9 dot puzzle. You have 3 sets of 3 dots and you have to connect them using 4 or less straight lines without lifting your pencil.

The solution involves literally 'thinking out of the box' and is below. I had first heard of this many years ago and every so often someone will bring it up and since I know the solution, I feel all cool and will quickly whip out the solution.

 

 

 

The other day, someone asked if I knew other solutions (not the one above). This gave me pause as I went from 'I know this' to 'hmmm.. never thought about a different solution'. Sure enough there are a couple other solutions and I am sure if one puts their mind to it, you can come up with many more.

It made me realize that there are often situations where I fall into this 'expert trap' where I jump to the solution I know and completely ignore other possibilities. There are cases where the solution I know, is probably the best one out there and it is much less time consuming to jump to the one I know rather than explore all possibilities. That being said, the trap I find myself falling into (at work and as a parent) is in not being able to distinguish between the situations where I do know the best option and situations where I don't know the best option, but act like I do.

Even something as trivial as playing Clash of Clans with my son. My natural tendency is to act like I know which attack strategy will work. To be truthful, I have a hunch that it will work but I act like it is 'The strategy'.

The other realization for me has been that sometimes letting others come to their own conclusion is best. Even if I know what the outcome will be, sometimes it is best to let people make their own mistakes and learn from them. It is so difficult to not say the 4 words that we all hate to hear - "I told you so".

So when my son tried a new attack strategy that I was sure was a bad idea, I kept my mouth shut and my eyes open. To my surprise it actually worked and he ended up teaching me something new and I had to hear those 4 words - "I told you so".
