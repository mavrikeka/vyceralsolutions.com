And you want to be my latex salesman?

Created on 2017-02-10 16:02

Published on 2017-02-10 16:41

I recently took on a new role, which means that after 10 years I am no longer in sales. Funny thing is that I feel I've learnt more about how sales really happen, in the last 2 months than I learnt in the last 10 years. The sobering thought is that what we do as a vendor is a small part of what really goes on.

I was pretty successful in sales and thought I had a really good understanding of how customers bought. I was good at building relationships, understanding customer needs, knowledgeable of how I could add value, organized about sequence of events, communicating often and early, disqualifying opportunities that were not real, challenging when needed etc. You get the point.

The last couple months I've been working on an internal project and I got exposed to the level of selling that happens internally. This came as a big surprise to me. I'd always known that my champions had to make the internal business case, get budget, get executives to approve etc. but that was often a black box to me and not something I actively focused on. I would hear tidbits of what went on but I was never privy to those conversations. My champions would surface with some objections (product, price, competition etc.) and I had to make sure to address those.

Which brings me to the headline of this post. We often talk about having the right champions at the account to be successful but it was never an area I spent too much time strategizing about. Oftentimes, I was stuck with the person I was dealing with and I worked hard to make them a champion.

The reality in sales (as I am realizing) is that I am dependent on someone at the other end to navigate the politics, culture, decision making process etc. within the organization. They have to talk to different groups that have various reasons not to go with my solution or stick with the status quo. If this person does not have the internal credibility, access to key decision makers, belief in my solution, willing to fight the tough internal battles, build consensus - chances are my ability to get the customer to change (and every sale is about change) will be affected.

So the next time you are in a sales cycle, think about the person on the other end you are dealing with and ask yourself - "can this person be my latex salesman?". Maybe that is why your deal is going sideways. More importantly, reach out to your champions that helped you be successful and thank them. There is a lot they do that you have no idea about.

How do you know if this person can be your latex salesman? Understand their 'Why'. Why is this important to them (personally and professionally)?
