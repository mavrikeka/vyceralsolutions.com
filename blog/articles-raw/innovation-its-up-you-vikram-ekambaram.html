(i)nnovation - It's up to you but what's stopping you

Created on 2017-05-20 12:12

Published on 2017-05-20 13:02

Our company had our annual meeting this week and one of the big themes was innovation and how every individual can be involved in innovation and that we should all be innovating in our jobs. Now this is easier said than done and there was no detail provided on how, we as individuals can innovate. Here is my attempt to get some thoughts on paper and get ideas from others on how we might go about innovating.

Over the last few months I've had the experience of working with a team to put into practice this concept of personal innovation - (i)nnovation. The (i) stands for both small innovations (you don't have to create the next Uber or AirBnB to innovate) and individual innovation (you don't need leadership to give you permission to innovate). It has been a great learning experience and a lot of fun. I've also blogged about parts of this concept in the past - Grassroots Innovation and Culture of innovation.

Before I get into what we learnt, I wanted to first define innovation. Funnily enough it is one of those words that is so overused now (along with disruption) that we all know what it is but we've created an aura around it and put it on a pedestal. Don't know about you but when I hear innovation I don't think it is something that I can do. If it was easy, wouldn't everyone be doing it and if everyone was doing it, it wouldn't be such a big deal, would it?

Here is what MW defines as innovation - A new idea, method or device OR the introduction of something new. When you put it this way, it doesn't seem that unattainable. We can all come up with a new idea or introduce something new. It doesn't say it has to change the world, it doesn't say it has to generate millions of dollars, it doesn't say only smart people sitting in their ivory towers can do this.

Now that we've got that out of the way, I'll start by sharing what stops me from (i)nnovating. If you don't take that first step, you are never going to (i)nnovate, so it is useful to understand what stops you from taking the first step before we get into how to do it.

  • The Aura of Innovation - It has to be something big otherwise it doesn't count
  • Fear of Failure - I don't want to invest all this time in something that might not work out.
  • Fear of Ridicule - It's not going to work out and my peers are going to make fun of me or judge me.
  • Lack of support from leadership - Nothing is more soul sucking than putting yourself out there, try something new, get some success but get no support from leadership. You end up with the 'Why bother' attitude. This takes a few different forms - someone above you usurps your idea, someone above you wants to compete with you rather than collaborate, someone above you just doesn't care, someone above you is too narrow-minded etc.
  • Waiting for Permission - Rather than take initiative, I am waiting for someone to ask me to do something.
  • Too busy - I am already working hard, how am I supposed to do yet another thing.
  • Too lazy - My personal favorite. Yes I can do this, but it is going to take too much effort. Inertia is too high.
  • Idea person - I love coming up with ideas but I want someone else to execute on them. A lot of us are idea people and don't have the stamina required to execute or see things through.
  • Perfectionist - I am not willing to do something unless it is just right. Sacrificing progress at the altar of perfection. Sadly, innovation is messy.
  • Give up - It is easy to start something, but really hard to see it through.
  • Clarity of next steps - I am a detail oriented person and need to have the next steps clearly defined before I start. Again, innovation is more of an agile and iterative process where you don't know what the next steps are when you begin.

I am sure there are many more and would love it if folks can share what stops them from (i)nnovating. The next post, will get into how the team went about innovating and the lessons we learned from that experience.
