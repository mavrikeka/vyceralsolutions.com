Expectations - They make or break you

Created on 2016-04-07 12:36

Published on 2016-04-07 15:28

Life it seems, is a game of expectations. A game where we are constantly graded or we constantly grade ourselves, on our ability to meet those expectations. The expectation quadrant above defines the two aspects of expectations - what is expected of you and what did you deliver. It does not matter if the expectations were created by others or if these are expectations you have of yourself, all that changes is who is grading you. Sometimes we are our toughest graders.

As long as we treat it as a game, we are good but we often tend to define ourselves by the labels in the game. A salesperson who overachieves on a big quota gets a bit too full of himself and starts believing that he is a superhero. A parent who is too busy working and does not have time for her family, feels she is a failure as she is not able to deliver on the expectations of her family.

For me, it is important to take a step back, rather than get caught up in the labels. This has helped me in sales - both in years where I didn't make my number (and felt like a failure) and in years I overachieved (and felt like a hero).

We also tend to make choices based on what we feel the outcome will be. Often we set low expectations so we can over deliver and be the hero, but this is a slippery slope to being perceived as irrelevant.

Does everyone have a default state? I think, I tend to fall in the under commit / over deliver bucket. Whenever I take on something new (new job, new role, new sport etc.) I tend to work twice as hard, because I know that it is easier to over achieve on the low expectations folks have. When I am doing a project, I tend to set expectations that are realistic and that I can deliver on. The downside is that I sometimes tend to play it safe.

Wonder if others have a default state and what strategies they use to handle the expectation labels?
