New Year's Resolution, Spinning Classes and Cold Calling

Created on 2015-12-22 14:43

Published on 2015-12-22 15:27

It is that time of the year, when I start thinking about my New Year's resolution(s) (NYR) and then very quickly stop thinking because I am reminded of the many I have made and not kept. I don't know why, but for some reason every NYR that I come up with is generally not fun. Not sure why we don't say - this year I will binge watch 12 shows or I will eat ice-cream every day. Those would be easy to keep. My big thing this year is to get some exercise.

I was thinking about similar work 'resolutions' - stuff we know we should do but find reasons not to. For me it is cold calling / emailing. It is such a high effort / low return activity that I would do it in bursts but never had fun.

Anyway, for the last couple months I have been noodling on how to get my lazy ass to the gym. I realized that the key was to create a routine. Otherwise I'd find excuses not to do it. A few weeks ago my wife mentioned that she was going to take a spinning class at the gym and I decided to join her. I have gone a few times over the last few weeks and I am hopeful that I will be able to keep it up. My NYR started a little early (why wait till Jan 1st).

What I learnt was that group exercise classes are great to help us create a new habit. In the Power of Habit, Charles Duhigg talks about the Habit Loop.

There is a Cue that kicks off a Routine and then there is a Reward. Over time you do things without even thinking. With group exercise classes, the Cue is that it is on a certain date on a certain time. Twice a week at the same time, you have the spinning class. The Routine is the spinning class and the Reward is feeling good. Now, if it was just me going to the gym to exercise, I'd come up with excuses (I'm tired, it's too late, it's raining etc.). The reason I am hopeful that this time is different is because my wife and I are making the joint commitment. So if I falter my wife will be there to push me along. Also, since it is a group exercise, you meet a group of people regularly and if my wife can't make it one day, I still have a larger group that I connect with.

Which brings me to cold-calling. The first step is to pick a certain time every day to make the calls. So the Cue is the time, the Routine is to make the calls and the Reward is leads / fulfillment etc. More important is to create a support system / community. Have a buddy who you can partner with that will be there for you when you just don't feel like making the cold calls. Maybe enroll your entire team and this becomes a team activity / competition. I picked cold calling as it is a lot of work for a little reward and if one does not create a 'habit' it is very easy to fall off the wagon, but this can apply to anything in life. So the next time you know you have to do something and you know it is not fun and you find yourself coming up with excuses, see if you can make it a 'habit' and enroll someone to buddy up with you.
